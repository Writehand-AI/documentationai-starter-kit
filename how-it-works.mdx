## The loop

Every Coterie runs on the same four-step cycle. Understanding it is the fastest way to understand the whole product.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit" icon="link">
    A member shares a link to something they've published — a post, an article, a design — into an open **party** (a scheduled engagement session). Submitting costs credit; a member who still owes engagement from a previous round can't submit until they've caught up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match" icon="shuffle">
    Coterie automatically groups participants and assigns each member a set of other members' posts to engage with — matched by niche, so the pairing actually makes sense.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Engage" icon="message-circle">
    Members work through their assignments before the party's window closes: real comments, real feedback, optionally rated. If something's off, they can report it instead of faking their way through it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Earn" icon="star">
    Completing an assignment earns credit and builds the member's reliability score. That credit is what buys their next submission — the loop closes and starts again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why a scheduled session, not an open feed

An always-open feed sounds more convenient, but it's exactly what kills engagement groups: nothing has a deadline, so nothing gets done. A **party** has a start, a window, and a close. Admins set how big it is and how long it runs; members know exactly when to show up and exactly what's expected of them while they're there.

A party moves through a simple lifecycle:

`scheduled → grouping → running → finished`

Once it's running, submissions lock, assignments go out, and the clock starts. When it closes, the results (completed assignments, credit changes, reliability updates) are final.

## Why credit, not goodwill

Every credit movement — earned by completing an assignment, spent by submitting a post — is recorded in an append-only ledger. That's what makes a member's balance auditable, and it's what makes "comment debt" enforceable rather than just a suggestion.

This is the mechanism that keeps a Coterie from turning into the same group chat everyone's already burned out on: you cannot keep taking without giving back, because the platform won't let you.

## Why niche matching matters

Every member picks a niche when they join. Assignments are built from within — and, where a creator enables it, explicitly *around* — that niche, so a marketer isn't stuck leaving feedback on a construction post they don't understand, and a competitor isn't quietly grading your homework.

## Reliability, not just participation

Showing up once doesn't make you reliable. Coterie tracks a reliability score per member based on how consistently they complete what they're assigned, plus a strike count for members who repeatedly skip or misbehave. Admins can pause a member whose reliability drops too far — the system protects the quality of the group, not just its size.

<Callout kind="tip">
  This is the whole model in one sentence: you get engagement in proportion to the engagement you give, on a schedule, tracked and enforced automatically.
</Callout>
