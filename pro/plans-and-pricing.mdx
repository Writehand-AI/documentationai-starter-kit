## What plans are for

Not every Coterie needs to charge, but plenty benefit from it. A paid tier signals commitment, filters for members who are serious, and gives you a way to gate deeper access behind a level of investment.

Money goes **directly to you**. Members pay into your own Stripe account, not Coterie's — you are the merchant of record, and your members never see Coterie in the transaction.

## Connecting Stripe

Before you can charge anyone, connect your Stripe account under **Admin → Payments**.

If you already have a Stripe account you can link it. If you don't, you'll create one during the flow. Either way Stripe will ask for real business and bank details and set up two-factor authentication — it's their process, not ours, and it takes a few minutes. Stripe may also hold a new account for review before enabling charges, so don't leave this until the day you launch.

Your Payments page shows **Ready to take payments** once Stripe has verified you. Until then, members can't be charged.

<Callout kind="info">
  Coterie takes **10% of each member payment on the Pro plan, and 0% on Standard**. Stripe's own processing fees apply on top, as they would with any Stripe account.
</Callout>

## Defining a plan

Each plan has a name, a price and interval, an optional short description, and a list of selling points. You can run up to ten, and mark any of them inactive to hide it from members without deleting it.

## What a plan includes

Three things a plan actually delivers, as opposed to the selling points, which are printed on your pricing page and enforce nothing:

| | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Sessions per month** | How many engagement sessions a member can join. Blank = unlimited. |
| **Collections this plan unlocks** | Which sets of tools the plan buys access to. Tick as many as you like. |
| **Assistant messages per month** | Per member. Blank falls back to your Coterie-wide default. |

The last two are **Pro features**, so they only appear if your Coterie is on Pro — a Standard Coterie has neither collections nor an assistant to include.

<Callout kind="warning">
  A collection ticked on **no plan at all** is open to every member. Empty is not locked. If you want to charge for a collection, it has to appear on at least one plan.
</Callout>

### The two allowances count differently, on purpose

**Sessions** are counted against the member's own billing period.

**Assistant messages** are counted per calendar month instead. The reason is that a member cancelling and resubscribing starts a *new* subscription with a new billing period — so a period-based count would start again. On a paid plan that's just buying more, which is fine. On a **free** plan it would be an unlimited reset for nothing, and the assistant runs on your own API key. A calendar month is a window nobody can move.

The practical upshot: a member upgrading mid-month gets the higher ceiling applied to what they've already used, rather than a fresh start.

## How members pay

Payment happens **after approval**, never before:

1. Someone applies to join
2. You approve them
3. They pick a plan and pay
4. They become a member

You never charge someone you might decline. If **How people get in** is set to *Join immediately*, the first two steps are skipped and they go straight to picking a plan. See [Joining & Access](/creators/joining-and-access) for the full picture.

Approvals expire after 30 days if the person doesn't complete payment.

## Free tiers alongside paid ones

You can offer a free plan next to paid ones. Someone choosing the free tier joins immediately with no payment step. This tends to work better than making the first interaction a payment decision — let people in, then give them a reason to move up.

A free plan can unlock collections and carry an assistant allowance like any other. "Has a plan" and "has paid" are different things here, and that's deliberate — a free tier that includes the basics is often the thing that makes the paid tier legible.

Members on **no plan at all** still get an assistant allowance: the Coterie-wide default in **Admin → Assistant**. Leaving that blank means unlimited, which on your own API key is worth a moment's thought.

## Structuring tiers well

The strongest plan structures map to something a member can clearly feel: more sessions, a collection of tools they actually want, more room with the assistant, access to a smaller inner circle. A paywall on exactly the same experience everyone else gets free is a hard sell, and tends to generate cancellations rather than upgrades.

Two or three tiers is usually plenty. Every extra tier is another decision you're asking someone to make before they've experienced anything.

## Managing payments and refunds

Everything after the sale — payouts, refunds, disputes, invoices — happens in **your own Stripe dashboard**, not in Coterie. That's the trade-off of being the merchant of record: you keep the customer relationship, and you handle the support that comes with it.
