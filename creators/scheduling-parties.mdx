## What a party is, from your side

A party is the unit of activity in your Coterie — every submission, every assignment, every bit of engagement your members experience happens inside one. Scheduling parties regularly is what turns a Coterie from a one-time setup into an actual running community.

## Scheduling one

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Parties" icon="calendar">
    From your admin dashboard, create a new party. It'll pre-fill from your [default settings](/creators/settings-and-party-rules) — size, window, room size — which you can override for this specific session.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the window" icon="clock">
    Decide how long members have to submit, and how long they then have to complete their assignments once grouping happens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish it" icon="send">
    Once scheduled, the party appears on members' Sessions calendars so they can opt in ahead of time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## While it's running

A party moves through a simple lifecycle: `scheduled → grouping → running → finished`. Once it moves into grouping, Coterie automatically builds rooms and assigns members to each other's posts, matched by niche. You don't need to manually pair anyone — the system handles it based on your niche and rotation settings.

Keep an eye on the party while it's running. If assignments are going uncompleted at scale, that's usually a sign your window is too tight, your party size is too big for the room size you've set, or comment debt has piled up and is quietly blocking submissions.

## Cadence

A community with parties running on a predictable rhythm — weekly, biweekly, whatever fits your niche — builds a habit. Sporadic, unpredictable scheduling is one of the fastest ways to lose momentum, since members stop checking for the next one.
