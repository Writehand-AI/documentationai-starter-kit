## Why niches exist

Niche is the single biggest lever you have over match quality. Too broad, and members get paired with people whose work they don't understand well enough to give useful feedback on. Too narrow, and you don't have enough people in any one niche to build reliable parties. Getting the balance right is most of the work of running a good Coterie.

## Defining your niches

Set up the niches members choose from during onboarding — these should map to how your actual audience thinks about itself, not an abstract taxonomy. If your community is "B2B marketers," niches like "content marketing," "paid acquisition," and "product marketing" will match better than one broad "marketing" bucket that dumps everyone together.

<Callout kind="tip">
  Start with fewer, broader niches than you think you need. You can always split a niche once you have enough members in it to support two smaller ones — merging an over-fragmented set later is harder.
</Callout>

## Matching mode

**Settings → Sessions → Niche matching** decides how much niche is used when circles are formed:

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Matched | Members are grouped with others in the same niche. The default. |
| Mixed | Niche is ignored — anyone can be grouped with anyone. |

Matched gives better feedback, because people understand each other's work. Mixed gives fuller circles, which matters more while your roster is small — a niche with four members in it can't fill a circle of fifteen.

## Changing a member's niche

Members pick their niche once, at onboarding, and can't change it themselves — if someone's focus has genuinely shifted, you'll need to update it for them from the admin dashboard.
