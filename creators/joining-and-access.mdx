## The three settings people mix up

Three separate switches decide how someone gets into your Coterie. They sound similar and they interact, so it's worth being precise about what each one actually does.

| Setting | Where it lives | What it controls |
|---|---|---|
| **List in Discover** | Settings → Discovery | Whether your Coterie appears in the Discover directory at all |
| **Open join** | Settings → Joining & Access | Whether people browsing Discover get a **Join** button or an **Apply** button |
| **Instant access** | Settings → Joining & Access | Whether your public apply page lets people in immediately, or sends you an application to review |

The important thing: **Open join and Instant access are two different doors.** Open join governs people who find you *inside the app* through Discover. Instant access governs people who arrive at your *public apply page* — usually from a link you shared. You can set them differently, and plenty of Coteries should.

<Callout kind="info">
  Open join only does anything while **List in Discover** is set to Public. If your Coterie is private, nobody is browsing to it in the first place, so the switch is disabled until you list it.
</Callout>

## Choosing your combination

**Fully open.** List in Discover: Public. Open join: On. Instant access: On. Anyone who finds you is in immediately, from either door. Good for a large, low-friction community where volume matters more than vetting.

**Vetted, but discoverable.** List in Discover: Public. Open join: Off. Instant access: Off. People can find you and ask to join, but you approve everyone. This is the default and it's the right one for most Coteries — the quality of a reciprocal-engagement circle depends on who's in it.

**Invite-only.** List in Discover: Private. Instant access: Off. You're not listed anywhere; the only way in is a link you send or an invitation. Good for a private cohort or a paid mastermind you fill yourself.

**Public front door, private directory.** List in Discover: Private. Instant access: On. You're not in the directory, but anyone with your apply-page link walks straight in. Useful when you're driving signups from a newsletter or a launch post and don't want directory traffic.

## What you ask applicants

On the same tab you can shape your apply page. Name and email are always asked and can't be turned off. Beyond that you can request a profile link, ask which niche fits them, ask why they'd be a good fit, and add up to five custom questions of your own.

Keep it short. Every extra question costs you applicants, and the ones you lose aren't reliably the ones you wanted to lose. Two or three well-chosen questions tell you more than a long form that people abandon halfway.

If you turn **Instant access** on, these questions still get asked — you just don't review the answers before the person joins. They're saved, so you can read them later.

## Paid membership

If you've set up [plans](/pro/plans-and-pricing), payment happens **after** approval, never before. The sequence is:

1. Someone applies
2. You approve them
3. They're notified, and they choose a plan and pay
4. They become a member

You're never charging someone you might turn down, and they never pay before knowing they're in. If you've enabled **Instant access**, step 1 and 2 are skipped and they go straight to choosing a plan.

<Callout kind="warning">
  Approvals expire after **30 days**. If someone doesn't complete their payment in that window they'll need to apply again. They get a reminder at day 25.
</Callout>

If your Coterie has no paid plans, none of this applies — approval alone makes someone a member.

### Free tiers alongside paid ones

You can run a free plan and paid plans side by side. Someone picking the free tier joins immediately at step 3, with no payment step. This is a good way to let people in the door and give them a reason to upgrade later, rather than making the very first interaction a payment decision.

## Inviting people directly

An invitation grants **approval**, not free access. If your Coterie has paid plans, someone you invite still picks a plan and pays like anyone else.

When you genuinely want to give someone free access — a co-founder, a first member, someone you owe a favour — tick **Give free access** on the invitation. That's recorded against your name in the audit log, so it's always clear who was comped and by whom.

This is deliberate. If invitations quietly bypassed payment, the invite button would become an accidental way to give away your own revenue.

## What happens to applications

Applications you never review expire after 90 days, and the applicant is told. Once an application reaches a final state — joined, declined, or expired — the personal details on it are removed after 12 months. Your application counts and conversion stats stay accurate; the personal data doesn't sit around longer than it's useful.
