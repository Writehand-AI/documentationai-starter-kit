## Why moderation matters here

The whole value of a Coterie rests on members trusting that the engagement they get is real. One member consistently phoning it in — copy-pasted comments, skipped assignments, inappropriate submissions — erodes that trust for everyone matched with them. Moderation is what protects the thing you're actually selling: genuine reciprocity.

## Reports

Members can report a post or an assignment that's off — inappropriate content, spam, someone clearly not engaging in good faith. Reports land in your queue for review; you decide what happens next, from a warning to a strike to removal.

## Strikes

Strikes accumulate against members who repeatedly skip assignments or get reported. Once a member crosses your [configured threshold](/creators/settings-and-party-rules), they're automatically paused — you don't have to catch every case manually, but you can always intervene sooner if something's clearly wrong.

## Reliability as an early signal

Before something becomes a strike-worthy problem, it usually shows up first as a dropping reliability score. Members who are falling behind on assignments but haven't yet triggered a strike are worth a proactive nudge — a quiet decline is easier to fix than a member who's already accumulated three strikes.

## Pausing and removing members

A paused member keeps their account and history but can't submit or receive new assignments until you reinstate them. This is the right first move for most issues — it protects the community without permanently cutting someone off for what might be a one-off lapse.
