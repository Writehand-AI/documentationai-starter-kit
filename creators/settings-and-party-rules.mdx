## Why this page matters

Every rule your members experience — how big a party is, how long they have to engage, how much debt is too much — comes from here. Get these right early, because changing them mid-stride tends to confuse a community more than launching with sensible defaults from day one.

## Content channels

Decide which platforms members can submit from — LinkedIn, blog, Instagram, and so on. Keep this tight to what your niche actually publishes on; allowing every channel under the sun just spreads your members' attention across formats they don't all care about.

## Party defaults

Set the shape of a typical session:

- **Party size** — how many members can join a single party.
- **Room size** — how members are broken into smaller groups within a party, if your community runs at scale.
- **Time window** — how long members have to submit and then complete their assignments before the party closes.

You can override these per-party when you [schedule one](/creators/scheduling-parties), but the defaults are what most sessions will run on.

## Comment-debt rules

This is the enforcement mechanism behind the whole platform: a member with unfinished assignments from a previous party can't submit into the next one. You control how strict that gate is and how strikes accumulate for members who habitually skip.

## Rotation

Control whether members get matched with the same people repeatedly or rotated through the wider community over time. Rotation keeps feedback varied; letting pairs repeat can build familiarity if that's what your community's culture is going for.

## Niche matching and competition-exclusion

Turn on competition-exclusion if your niche is one where members might genuinely compete for the same clients or audience — it keeps direct competitors out of each other's assignment pool. See [Managing Niches](/creators/managing-niches) for how niches themselves are structured.

## Strike threshold

Set how many strikes a member can accumulate — missed assignments, reported behavior — before they're automatically paused. Pair this with your [moderation](/creators/moderation-and-reports) process for anything that needs a human judgment call instead of an automatic one.
